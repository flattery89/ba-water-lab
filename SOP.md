# Standard Operating Procedure — Water Lab

How to use the Brewers Anonymous Water Lab, from source water to brew day.
(The same procedure is built into the app: tap **SOP** in the header, or **▶ Tutorial** for a guided walkthrough.)

## Before you start

- Have your **source water report** in ppm (Ca, Mg, Na, SO₄, Cl, HCO₃) — or plan to brew with RO / distilled.
- Know your **grain bill** (each malt, weight, color in °L) and your **mash** and **sparge** volumes.
- Keep a **pH meter** handy for brew day — the app predicts mash pH, but you confirm it.

## Procedure

1. **Source water.** Pick a preset or type your ppm. If your tap is hard, drag **Dilute with RO**; the Water split section then shows how much RO and tap to mix in each vessel.
2. **Target profile.** Choose a Modern style or Classic city profile, or enter Custom numbers.
3. **Grain bill.** Add each malt and weight. Colors auto-fill for known malts but can be edited to match your maltster.
4. **Water split & targets.** Enter mash and sparge volumes, target mash pH, sparge pH, salt strategy, and (once calibrated) the system calibration.
5. **On the shelf.** Check the salts you actually own and select your acid.
6. **Calculate.** The solver fills the additions for each vessel.
7. **Review.** Compare the Final wort profile to target and read any **guardrail warnings** at the top — they flag unreachable targets and the fix (e.g. enable Slaked Lime, raise RO dilution).
8. **Fine-tune.** Every salt amount is editable per vessel — nudge any value and the resulting ppm and mash pH update live.
9. **Save.** Name the recipe and **Save .csv** to your device (or Load one back).
10. **Brew day.** Open the **Brew-day sheet**, print it or keep it on your phone, and tick off each step: mix RO/tap per the blend guide, weigh salts into the correct vessel, stir in acid, and **verify mash pH with your meter**. Adjust the calibration slider next time so predictions match your system.

## Good to know

- Mash pH is an **estimate** — always confirm with a meter and calibrate to your system.
- Classic city profiles are **reference points, not prescriptions**. For dark beers, let roasted grain provide acidity rather than chasing high bicarbonate.
- Alkalinity salts (baking soda, slaked lime) always go in the **mash**, never the sparge.
